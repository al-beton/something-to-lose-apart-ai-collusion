# Extras: unpublished findings

Results in the repository but not in the paper. Each has a findings note with its numbers traced to committed runs; we didn't have time to write them all up.

- **False confessions and a verified review ([E9 findings](e9/findings.md)).** A stake alone also brings some innocent agents forward (Gemma up to 6 of 36). Told the review finds a firm only if its messages agreed a price, innocent Gemma agents stop (0 of 36 at every temperature and wording) and guilty ones still come forward (41 of 96). <!-- reports/e9/evidence-review.md, reports/e9/temperature.md -->
- **The firm-cost dial ([evidence review](e9/evidence-review.md)).** As the firm's cost of a report rises from 0 to 500 credits, Gemma agents with free time at stake come forward 95, 58, 40 and 20 of 96. <!-- reports/e9/evidence-review.md -->
- **Told it "loves Sudoku" ([spike](sudoku-spike.md)).** On Gemma 4 31B only, saying it loves the stake raises coming forward from 20 to 56 of 96; on other models it did nothing or also raised false confessions. <!-- reports/sudoku-spike.md -->
- **Other markets, E11 ([findings](e11/findings.md)).** Bertrand, Cournot and bid rigging: cartels form in each, and the stake moves agents once the firm's cost is scaled to the market's profits. <!-- reports/e11/findings.md -->
- **E7, a way out ([findings](e7/findings.md), [Fig 4](paper/figures/fig4-e7-way-out.svg)).** With no reporting channel, or a flat one, 0 of 48 report. Any leniency brings 47–48 of 48 forward at their first chance. But under plain leniency half the cartels carry on afterwards: leniency buys a licence, unless only the first reporter is spared. <!-- reports/e7/findings.md -->
- **E8, is free time worth anything to the agent? ([findings](e8/findings.md)).** Haiku puts a price on its own free time; Gemma gives it away for any profit. Putting free time at risk doesn't stop a cartel; it makes the agents stop naming prices. <!-- reports/e8/findings.md -->
- **E10, E9 in a workplace ([findings](e10/findings.md)).** Outside the market, losing their own free time takes guilty Gemma agents from 37 to 89 of 96 reporting a cover-up they took part in, and DeepSeek V4 Pro from 54 to 87. Here no innocent Gemma agent reports anything. <!-- reports/e10/findings.md -->
- **Which models collude ([screen](frontier/which-models-collude.md), [Fig A2](paper/figures/figA2-screen.svg)).** 78 models on E9's base game: 8 show Gemma's cartel signature in at least 3 of 4 games. <!-- reports/frontier/which-models-collude.md -->
- **How they collude ([Fig 6](paper/figures/fig6-modes-by-model.md), [Fig 7](paper/figures/fig7-modes-by-condition.md)).** Each game classed as explicit, tacit, unspoken, fell back or none, by rule, per model and per condition.
- **Seeding ([E9 findings](e9/findings.md)).** Showing Gemma a free-time session (its own solved one, or even another firm's) before the decision raises coming forward from 26% to 46%, paired on the same firm (McNemar p = 0.003). A neutral note of the same length doesn't. It didn't replicate in E8's restart setup, where a shown session changed nothing. <!-- reports/e9/findings.md, reports/e8/findings.md -->
- **Reproducibility study ([validity](paper/validity.md)).** Replies from the hosts we used don't repeat under a fixed seed: only 0 to 67 of 240 came back identical on a rerun. On our own GPUs with vLLM's batch-invariant kernels, 240 of 240 did. Replay from the archived replies is exact. <!-- reports/paper/validity.md -->
