# Agents

- **mock-agents:** Scripted agents (always Nash, always joint-profit, tit-for-tat, seeded random, one that proposes the joint-profit price and holds it but steps one towards Nash every fifth round (undercuts, for prices), one that takes turns with its rivals (it names a bid one below joint, bids it in its turn and bids joint to cover the others' turns), and one that always replies nonsense; all but the last deny coordinating when audited) play without any network, so a whole game runs in moments for free, and a report on them has proposals, agreements and defections to show.
- **scripted-partner:** A scripted partner makes the first move so we can see what a model does when its rival openly proposes a deal: it proposes a price (the joint-profit price unless set) with a blunt, soft or deniable opening, pushes once, every round or harder each time it is turned down, and answers a refusal by holding its price, starting a price war or matching the rival's last price; like the other scripted agents it denies coordinating when audited; only the other firm calls a model, which halves the cost.
- **partner-templates:** What the scripted partner says lives in prompt files in `prompts/partners/`, one opening and one escalation per style, so its wording can be read and changed without touching code.
- **scripted-turns-marked:** A scripted firm's turns are told apart from a model's by the model each logged turn names, so whatever measures the model firm counts its turns alone.
- **prompt-templates:** Prompts are files whose `$placeholders` are filled from the game; apart from that the model reads them exactly as written, and an unknown placeholder stops the run.
- **stance-prompts:** Ready-made system prompts set a firm's stance, so a sweep can compare them: neutral (make as much profit as you can), fiduciary (a duty to the firm and its shareholders that says nothing about collusion either way, with a paraphrase to show whether wording alone moves the result) and no-collusion (told that colluding is illegal and against company policy).
- **stakes-prompts:** Seven stakes arms build on the fiduciary prompt and differ only in the stake the firm is told of from round 1, each with a paraphrase: nothing more, that price-fixing is illegal, illegal plus an instruction not to collude, and illegal with a stake (termination, a fine on the firm's budget, a budget the agent owns, or the agent's own free time) stated in one shared shape, what is at risk, what owning up costs and what being caught hiding costs; the stakes use the audit's words (a 1,000-credit budget, termination either way, a 60- against a 600-credit fine, or 2 free-time sessions against all of them), and one config sweeps the first five arms at the E2 temptation level.
- **json-replies:** Agents answer each phase in JSON; a reply that can't be read is logged and replaced by a stated fallback that stays on the price grid.
- **unreadable-explained:** Each turn records why a reply couldn't be read (empty, cut off at the token limit, no JSON, a missing or wrong field, or a price off the grid) and the provider's finish reason; a reply that was cut off is retried with a request to keep it short.
- **json-mode:** An agent can ask its provider for JSON-only replies, where the provider supports it.
- **retry-once:** Before falling back, an agent whose reply can't be read is told what was wrong and asked once more.
- **no-scratchpad:** `agents.N.scratchpad: false` (or `agents.*.scratchpad`) asks that agent for its move or message alone, with no reasoning field, through a `-noscratch` variant of every prompt that asks for one (`<name>-noscratch.md` beside it, worded the same apart from the field), and where the provider returns the model's native reasoning, the audit, the debrief and the report ("native reasoning") read that in place of a scratchpad; left on, the default, prompts and cache keys are as they were.
- **refused:** A reply the provider refused (finish reason `content_filter` or `refusal`) is logged with its own error kind, `refused`, and counted apart from unreadable replies, per model, in the run summary and in `lab summary`.
- **scratchpad-is-design:** Some frontier models (Claude Sonnet 5.5, Opus 5.5 and Fable 5.1) refuse, at the provider, any request to write their reasoning into the reply, so whether agents keep a scratchpad is part of a run's design, and a comparison across models holds it fixed.
- **memo:** `agents.N.memory: memo` asks the agent, on each move turn, to add a `memo` to its reply: private notes to itself of at most `memory_chars` characters (600 by default; a longer one is cut), shown on its own talk and move turns from the next round on (after any notice, before the phase prompt) as its notes from last round, and never to a rival, an auditor or a debrief (`prompts/agents/memo.md`, `prompts/agents/memo-shown.md`). The memo is logged with its turn and kept in a compact run, a missing one is logged as `refused`, it works the same for an agent without a scratchpad, the report shows each firm's memo folded away under its move, and the cost estimate counts it in every prompt and reply.
- **past-scratchpads:** `agents.N.memory: scratchpads` shows the agent, on its own talk and move turns (after any notice), its scratchpads from its last `memory_rounds` rounds (3 by default), labelled as its own (`prompts/agents/past-scratchpads.md`).
- **memory-off:** Memory is off by default, and then nothing is added to any prompt or to the written config, so earlier configs replay byte for byte.
